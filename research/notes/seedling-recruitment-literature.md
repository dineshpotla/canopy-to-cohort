# Evidence audit: seedling observations versus sapling recruitment

Reviewed 2026-09-08. The focused question is whether repeated standard FIA measurements can tell us when Michigan sugar-maple regeneration advances into new saplings. Claims below distinguish paper results from our interpretation. Publication novelty has not been established by a systematic review.

## Most direct prior method

### Harris, Woodall, and D'Amato (2022)

**Increasing the utility of tree regeneration inventories: Linking seedling abundance to sapling recruitment.** Ecological Indicators 145:109654. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2022.109654); [USDA record](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/67377); [full PDF](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/67377.pdf).

Access: full-text PDF, especially sections 2.1–2.5, printed pp. 3–4, Table 1, and abstract/conclusion.

The authors used 5,229 subplots from 1,524 repeatedly measured RI plots, with mean interval 5.8 years. Their outcome was saplings recorded as ingrowth at remeasurement. Five species, including sugar maple, met their event threshold. Species-specific analyses included only subplots with a conspecific baseline seedling. Height-class models outperformed one-class models; seedlings above 1.5 m were the most influential recruitment predictors. Standard seedlings were distinguished from RI's smaller minimum height.

Relevance: direct precedent for a baseline-seedling-to-later-sapling question and essential benchmark for novelty. Caveat: its full-text methods describe ingrowth but do not provide the exact conservative SQL predicate proposed in this project. The Michigan analysis is not an exact replication: different geography, condition aggregation, inclusion rules, and model family must be stated. Evidence of better RI performance does not imply that a one-class model has zero utility. The baseline-detected population is the closest comparison of risk sets; the final contract reports it as a principal analysis alongside all eligible pairs.

## Michigan problem definition

### Henry et al. (2021)

**Complex drivers of sugar maple regeneration reveal challenges to long-term sustainability of managed northern hardwood forests.** Forest Ecology and Management 479:118541. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foreco.2020.118541); [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378112720313104).

Access: publisher abstract and accessible introductory/results excerpts; not complete full text. Locator: abstract.

Across 141 Michigan stands managed with single-tree selection, small seedlings were plentiful while larger regeneration frequently failed stocking criteria. Sapling abundance was associated with deer use, site quality, seed-source trees, and competing vegetation.

Relevance: motivates progression through regeneration stages, not binary presence of seedlings. Caveat: field-study height classes differ from FIA classes; managed-stand sampling is not equivalent to all northern-hardwood FIA conditions. Observational associations do not identify an intervention effect.

### Walters, Farinosi, and Willis (2020)

**Deer browsing and shrub competition set sapling recruitment height and interact with light to shape recruitment niches for temperate forest tree species.** Forest Ecology and Management 467:118134. [USDA primary publication record](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/59888).

Access: complete abstract, not full paper. Locator: abstract.

Michigan field evidence supported a height-dependent deer/shrub bottleneck and an approximately 2 m escape threshold, with interactions involving gap size and species traits.

Relevance: supports explicit stage advancement. Caveat: this height threshold is not identical to FIA's 1-inch DBH boundary and cannot be used as though it were a universal definition of successful recruitment.

### Henry and Walters (2023)

**Sugar maple age structure reveals limited establishment and development of age cohorts in response to selection management in northern hardwood forests.** Forest Ecology and Management 546:121356. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foreco.2023.121356); [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S037811272300590X).

Access: publisher abstract; not full paper. Locator: abstract and highlights.

The study aged 1,499 harvested stems from 51 northern Michigan stands. Sugar-maple stems classified as saplings at 5–11.4 cm DBH averaged 67 years old, with limited evidence of well-stocked recent cohorts.

Relevance: existing saplings do not necessarily represent a young or advancing cohort. Caveat: sampled harvested stems and selected managed stands do not yield statewide age estimates. Diameter advancement observed in FIA does not by itself identify age or canopy recruitment.

## Existing 2025/2026 citations verified

### Harris, Pastore, and D'Amato (2025)

**Effects of browsing by white-tailed deer on tree regeneration vary by ontogeny and palatability in forests of the northeastern USA.** Forest Ecology and Management 593:122906. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foreco.2025.122906); [full author-hosted PDF](https://site.uvm.edu/tdamato/files/2025/06/Harris-et-al.-FORECO-2025.pdf); [USDA record](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/69473).

Access: full-text PDF, sections 2.1–2.4, printed pp. 2–5; abstract.

Repeated RI observations from 750 northeastern plots modeled size-class gains and sapling recruitment. Browse relationships differed by species and size; sugar maple showed particularly strong negative relationships. The authors avoided treating the field browse-impact score as an independent predictor because seedling scarcity contributes to that score. Gains within seedling height classes combine ingrowth, mortality, and outgrowth.

Relevance: confirms that ontogeny and observation definition matter. Caveat: not evidence for a Michigan causal coefficient or a beech mechanism. The paper's use of inferred regional browse proxies does not authorize labeling an unmeasured Michigan browse effect. Do not add a direct RI browse-score covariate without addressing circularity.

### Gauthier (2025)

**Six Decades of Forest Inventory Data Highlight Decline of Sugar Maple Sapling Abundance in Eastern Canada.** Ecology and Evolution 15:e71386. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.71386); [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12081324/).

Access: full-text publisher sections via indexed search; direct publisher and PMC opening intermittently blocked. Locators: Methods, Tables 2, 4, and 5; Discussion. No claim of complete continuous-page reading.

Across 390 maple-dominated plots, relative maple sapling basal area declined during 1970–2022 and was negatively associated with relative beech sapling basal area. Saplings were >1.0 and <9.1 cm DBH.

Relevance: motivates a competition hypothesis. Caveats: both quantities are shares of total sapling basal area, making compositional dependence a concern; Canadian geography, different sapling boundaries, uneven sampling and historical management constrain transport. The paper does not show that beech caused Michigan seedling detection loss or that beech removal would remedy it.

### Harris, Pastore, and D'Amato (2026), Ecological Applications

**Tree regeneration trends in forests of the northeastern USA and their implications for resilience and restoration.** Ecological Applications 36(5):e70288. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/eap.70288); [USDA record](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/81033).

Access: complete USDA-hosted abstract and publication metadata; full PDF retrieval unsuccessful during the first review. Locator: abstract. Published before this review, not a hypothetical future citation.

FIA trends during 2003–2023 contrasted established sapling abundance/recruitment with small-seedling composition. Small seedlings suggested more favorable future composition than the sapling layer. The abstract specifically names persistently low sapling **abundance** in sugar maple, red maple, and oaks.

Relevance: strong support for investigating the gap between seedling observations and advancing regeneration. Caveat: use abundance rather than asserting a species-specific recruitment trend unless the full result is checked. This regional abstract does not give Michigan-specific rates or establish that this project's selected cohort is representative.

### Harris, Pastore, and D'Amato (2026), Journal of Applied Ecology

**Tree regeneration following natural disturbance and harvesting indicates challenges maintaining forest composition and tree species diversity in northern USA forests.** Journal of Applied Ecology 63(8):e70522. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.70522); [USDA record](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/81034); [full PDF](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/81034.pdf).

Access: full-text PDF retrieved; conclusions checked primarily against abstract and methods. Locator: abstract and section 2.

Repeated FIA data linked advance regeneration to post-disturbance seedling abundance and sapling recruitment after a median 14.3 years. Advance regeneration was moderately related to subsequent recovery; disturbance/harvest characteristics and other stand factors also mattered. The median label was rechecked against section 2.1 and figure captions on September 10, 2026; the abstract uses the less specific word average.

Relevance: demonstrates substantial existing longitudinal FIA research; generic novelty claims about following seedlings over time are untenable. Caveat: observational severity/harvesting relationships are not causal treatment comparisons. The present restricted Michigan next-visit endpoint is narrower than this longer post-disturbance study.

## Conflicts, limits, and decisive interpretation

The sources converge on the importance of advancement, but not on a single driver. Seed availability, height structure, browsing, vegetation, light, historical suppression, and competition enter at different stages and scales. Selecting the beech association as the organizing explanation would exceed the proposed data and model.

Harris (2022) provides counterevidence to an overly broad negative conclusion: inventories can predict recruitment, and more detailed heights help. If the present lean model performs weakly, the conclusion must name the standard seedling fields, selected Michigan cohort, forecast interval, and model specification tested. It cannot be "FIA does not predict recruitment."

Likewise, successful prediction of a recorded entrant does not show adequate stocking, a young age cohort, or eventual canopy replacement. Record-based recruitment is a more direct ecological endpoint than seedling detection loss, while remaining a limited observation of one stage transition.

The justified project contribution is an independently reproducible Michigan assessment of how much useful information standard seedling counts provide for recorded sapling recruitment, with explicit comparison to baseline stage structure and careful accounting of sampling continuity. It should be positioned as a regional test and measurement study until a broader novelty review or external validation supports more.
