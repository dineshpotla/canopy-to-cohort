# Coverage and competing explanations

| Question | Evidence required | Test that could change the conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Are apparent entrants genuine new sapling tallies on comparable area? | FIADB guide by protocol, TREE reconciliation, plot kind/QA, microplot change matrix and TREE subplot | Removing newly observed area or ambiguous codes substantially changes event support. |
| Do baseline seedlings anticipate recruitment beyond observation opportunity? | Matched design-only versus count-model held-out predictions | Little or negative Brier improvement, poor calibration, or unstable geographic transfer. |
| Does existing maple stage structure add useful information? | Fixed count versus stage comparison in both analysis populations | Stage variables fail to improve the count benchmark or improve only an easy baseline-zero stratum. |
| Can apparent seedling abundance give an incomplete picture? | Absolute entry/no-entry counts by fixed abundance bins and linked sapling fates | High abundance consistently predicts recorded entry well; no evidence supports a blanket false-reassurance narrative. |
| Does sapling disappearance mean mortality? | Tagged live/dead/removed/outgrown/corrected/unlinked records | Living outgrown stems or identity changes explain some size-class disappearance. |
| Is this a novel general FIA method? | Harris (2022) and subsequent regional recruitment studies | Existing direct precedent limits novelty to a Michigan evaluation and specific comparisons. |

## Contradictions versus differences in scope

- Plentiful seedlings and low later sapling entry can both be true. They are
  different stage measurements, not contradictory population estimates.
- Baseline count can predict recruitment and still leave most conditions with
  no recorded entrant. Predictive association and high absolute no-entry
  frequency can coexist when the endpoint is uncommon.
- A seedling-detection AUC of 0.856 and a weaker or stronger recruitment AUC are
  not a head-to-head contest: the outcomes and eligible populations differ.
- RECONCILECD 1 is not sufficient by itself: newly forested sampling area is a
  competing explanation. Full condition proportions and microplot-center
  labels do not locate every tree within a changing boundary.
- Several publications reuse FIA observations. They establish methodological
  precedent and regional context but are not independent replications of this
  project's Michigan record-level calculation.

The report must allow both positive and negative evidence. Failure to locate a
contrary paper is not itself proof that a hypothesis is correct.
