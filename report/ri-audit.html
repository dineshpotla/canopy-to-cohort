Regeneration length classes and measurement feasibility

Michigan FIA regeneration-indicator sampling, count reconciliation, and support for a height-information comparison.

Findings and direction

The Michigan regeneration-indicator (RI) records support a descriptive measurement study, but not a strong new forecasting claim. The baseline sampling and record checks retain 90 condition intervals on 87 physical plots, with 14 recruit-bearing conditions. The appropriate next scientific question is whether seedling length distribution adds information beyond ordinary counts on the same sampled area, not whether another stand variable can improve a headline metric.

Taller seedlings provide a plausible lead. Among 44 conditions with RI maple seedlings at least five feet long, 13 had recorded new saplings at follow-up; among 46 without such seedlings, 1 did. This is a retrospective association in an already explored sample. It is not a held-out comparison with seedling abundance, proof of individual survival, or a management threshold.

The information constraint remains decisive: the existing later-year split would provide 10 development events and 4 assessment events. No height-class model is fitted. The current contribution is a reconciled measurement audit and a more specific testable hypothesis; evidence for an operational survey tool remains absent.

Endpoint and population

The source population is the 922 eligible intervals from the recruitment study, not all Michigan forest conditions. The outcome remains a new live sugar-maple sapling tally at follow-up, using the existing species, diameter, reconciliation, predecessor, and shared-sampling checks. No eligibility rule is relaxed to obtain more RI records or events.

Baseline RI is a potential predictor. Follow-up RI is a different measurement and is not needed to establish baseline length structure when the follow-up TREE endpoint is otherwise valid. Requiring RI at both visits would answer a narrower question and could discard valid baseline observations. Neither condition intervals nor sampled microplots are whole management stands, and these results are not statewide, survey-weighted population estimates.

Sampling and protocol definitions

The database dictionary retires REGEN_SUBP_STATUS_CD from inventory year 2015 and identifies REGEN_MICR_STATUS_CD as the status field populated across years. Code 1 identifies sampled RI on accessible forest; code 2 is sampled but without accessible forest on the microplot. Codes 3-5 are nonsampled and code 9 is historically ambiguous. A blank retired field is therefore not a reason to reject an otherwise valid code-1 forest sample. Conversely, code 2 cannot verify RI coverage of a positive forest-condition area. (Burrill et al. 2025, sec. 6.2.10 and 6.2.22)

The baseline screen requires RI plot presence, code-1 coverage of every contributing condition-microplot slice, consistent subplot identifiers, compatible core sampling, and a reconstructed condition area matching the existing microplot proportion. RI seedling records must have valid condition and subplot-condition foreign keys, species/source/length codes, and counts. Duplicate keys stop the calculation; invalid records make the affected plot’s RI measurements unavailable. Missing species tallies are not zero-filled until the measurement frame passes.

The inspected annual supplements specify a May-September RI window and established seedlings at least two inches long, below one inch DBH. Length is measured along the seedling from base to terminal bud, not necessarily vertical height. Counts above five may be estimated. Stump sprouts and suckers from one origin are not independent stems; only the tallest qualifying stump sprout is tallied. These details limit biological interpretations of the count totals. (U.S. Forest Service 2016, sec. 6.0 and 6.4; U.S. Forest Service 2018, 18–22)

The earlier RI technical report documents the same length schema and the regional inventory’s development. It does not substitute for reading every annual field supplement. (McWilliams et al. 2015, 18–23 and 72)

Historical coverage and date limitations

The full relevant sections of the 2016, 2017, and 2018 annual supplements were inspected. These years cover 37 available baseline intervals with four events. The 53 older intervals have dictionary and historical technical-report support, but their individual 2012-2015 annual supplements were not recovered. Full version-by-version protocol equivalence is not certified.

The official archive’s 2016 copy is marked DRAFT. Its length boundaries agree with the inspected 2017/2018 supplements and dictionary, but a final deployed 2016 edition is not certified. Older generic cross-references also retain inconsistent subplot/status wording; the audit uses the explicit microplot status table and dictionary. These qualifications are retained in the protocol note.

PLOT.MANUAL is not treated as a precise P2+ supplement edition: for example, baseline records store 7.0 while the available annual supplements include 7.1 and 7.2. Year-specific documentary coverage is recorded separately. Plot measurement dates provide a plausibility screen, not proof of the exact RI measurement day or leaf condition; the RI tables expose no separate field date.

Eligibility and valid zeros

All 90 baseline records pass the implemented frame, record, and date checks. They cover 311 contributing microplot-condition intervals and have exact mapped overlap in the existing recruitment frame. This is a qualified local eligibility finding, not independent certification of all field measurements. The other 832 eligible recruitment intervals have no baseline RI plot record; their length-class values remain unavailable, not zero.

At follow-up, two intervals fail the RI frame checks and two more have plot dates outside the RI window, leaving 86 under a both-visit restriction. These four flags do not invalidate their baseline RI or the existing TREE endpoint. The date flags require clarification before using follow-up RI to estimate length-class changes.

The 19 conditions without ordinary baseline seedling detections split into nine with no qualifying RI maple tally and ten containing only smaller RI seedlings. Thus an ordinary zero is not equivalent to an all-size RI zero. None of those 19 had the defined recorded entry in this snapshot; that does not imply that small seedlings can never advance or that recruitment cannot follow a zero.

Count reconciliation

The ordinary maple count equals the sum of RI classes 3-6 in every one of the 311 matched microplot-condition intervals and all 90 condition intervals. Both totals are 2,658. Including the two smaller RI classes raises the recorded total to 9,489; four of those tallies use the stump-source category.

This establishes numerical redundancy of the collapsed counts in this sample, not independence of the two measurements. Shared or derived tally information is a plausible explanation for exact agreement; the database-generation code has not been audited, so a specific derivation mechanism is not asserted. It would be misleading to count agreement as independent field validation.

The candidate additional information is the distribution among length classes and the smaller-size counts. Comparing all 9,489 RI tallies with the 2,658 ordinary tallies as though they measured the same size range would confound measurement definition with information gain. Neither denominator supports an individual seedling-to-sapling survival rate.

Descriptive length support

The at-least-five-foot grouping was fixed before calculating these associations, based on the published RI schema and the recruitment literature’s emphasis on taller seedlings. Harris and colleagues already showed that length classes can improve recruitment prediction in a larger regional FIA sample; the present Michigan result is not a novel demonstration of that general idea. (Harris et al. 2022)

The primary contrast is 29.5% versus 2.2%. Restricting to the 71 ordinary-detected conditions removes the easiest ordinary-zero group, but still does not adjust for differences in counts, interval length, site, or existing maple stages. A tall seedling is already closer in size to the recorded sapling boundary; this association must not be reinterpreted as a causal effect of height or browsing.

The intervals resample whole counties 2,000 times without fitting a prediction model. The one-event group is particularly fragile: its bootstrap distribution can contain no events, so a zero lower bound is not evidence of zero underlying risk. The intervals omit question selection, historical-protocol uncertainty, measurement error, and uncertainty about the intended survey decision.

Forecasting feasibility and next decision

A development/assessment split following the existing calendar boundary has 53 development intervals with ten events and 37 later intervals with four events, with no shared physical plots across the split. One missed assessment event would change observed event recall by 25 percentage points. These already explored outcomes are not an untouched validation set even if their length predictors were previously unused.

As a precision illustration only, even a hypothetical rule finding all 14 event plots would have an exact two-sided binomial 95% lower recall bound of 76.8% under independent-event sampling. This is not the observed rule’s recall, a clustered confidence interval, or a sample-size justification. Geographic dependence and a held-out sample would further constrain what can be learned.

The evidence supports retaining length structure as the next measurement hypothesis. It does not support a six-class fitted forecasting model or an operational score from this subset. A useful next study would fix a single supportable length representation, retain ordinary counts and sampling area as comparators on identical cases, and evaluate the paired gain in a separate, adequately supported inventory sample. A meaningful gain, forecasting horizon, decision target, action unit, and measurement cost must be specified first.

Within the existing Michigan snapshot, the baseline measurement milestone is complete with the historical-access qualifications above. The defensible contribution is the distinction between seedling abundance, length structure, and recorded sapling entry, together with explicit limits on usefulness. Acquiring other inventories or coordinating field work requires a separate scope decision; neither has occurred. The survey validation plan records these gates.

The subsequent count-versus-length design fixes a one-term comparison and planning scenarios. Its outcome-free historical opportunity screen finds only six physical plots beyond the current recruitment cohort among positive-seedling opportunities, before full eligibility checks. It does not establish a new independent Michigan test sample.

Reproducibility

Run make ri-audit after the recruitment dataset exists. The eligibility table, matched-count results, length support, and split support contain the aggregate evidence. Record-level joins remain local and Git-ignored.

The audit contract, protocol evidence note, and claim review distinguish declared analytical rules, documentary access, local results, and limitations.
