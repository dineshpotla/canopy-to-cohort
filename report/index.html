Abstract

This reproducible study analyzes 1,457 plot-condition measurements from Michigan FIA’s maple/beech/birch forest-type group. The primary inferential cohort requires demonstrated seedling sampling and at least one live sugar-maple TREE record with diameter at breast height (DBH) of 5 inches or more, leaving 1,072 conditions across 66 counties. It separates established-tree basal area (DBH ≥ 5 inches), sapling presence (DBH 1–4.9 inches), and seedling detection. In raw comparisons, seedling non-detection was 45.7% without sugar-maple saplings and 18.1% with them. In a county random-intercept logistic model, sapling presence remained strongly associated with lower non-detection odds, whereas established-tree basal area alone was not independently precise. Deterministic 10-fold validation held out entire counties and produced a Brier score of 0.177 and ROC AUC of 0.749. A full-cohort nonlinear model is retained only as context because it combines sapling basal area with established trees and therefore mixes species absence with regeneration continuity. Results are cross-sectional sample associations, not FIA population estimates, transitions, or causal effects.

1 Research question

Northern hardwood stands may retain established sugar maple while younger size classes are sparse. A single all-size basal-area variable can hide that structure because FIA TREE records include both 1–4.9-inch saplings and trees at least 5 inches DBH. The analysis therefore asks:

Among seedling-sampled Michigan northern-hardwood conditions with established sugar maple, which stand, cohort, management, and broad climate characteristics are associated with a zero sugar-maple seedling tally?

The direction is deliberately bounded. It studies contemporary cohort continuity, not seedling-to-canopy survival through time.

2 Data and population

2.1 Forest Inventory and Analysis

The Michigan SQLite release comes from the U.S. Forest Service FIA DataMart (U.S. Forest Service 2026). The release pins the current EXPCURR evaluation “MICHIGAN 2025: 2019-2025: CURRENT AREA, CURRENT VOLUME” (EVALID 262501; inventory window 2019–2025). FIA reference tables resolve sugar maple and retained forest types rather than relying on unexplained numeric codes (Burrill et al. 2025).

The analytical key is a plot visit and condition (PLT_CN + CONDID). TREE and SEEDLING records are aggregated independently before joining, which prevents a tree-by-seedling Cartesian product.

2.2 Climate and geography

Daymet Version 4 R1 supplies 1991–2020 temperature and precipitation normals (Thornton et al. 2022). Each county uses one interior representative Daymet cell. These are broad county proxies, not plot exposures.

Exact FIA plot locations are confidential. The analysis neither uses nor infers them. County identifiers are the only spatial link, so the maps show aggregated sample support and no plot-level spatial residual claim is made.

3 Methods

3.1 Size classes and basal area

For every live TREE record, plot-basis basal-area contribution is

\[ BA_i = 0.005454 \times DIA_i^2 \times TPA_{i,unadj}. \]

Condition density is then constructed record by record using the proportion for that record’s sampling frame:

\[ BA_c = \sum_{i \in c}\frac{0.005454 \times DIA_i^2 \times TPA_{i,unadj}} {PROP_{c,frame(i),unadj}}. \]

Trees below 5 inches use MICRPROP_UNADJ; trees at least 5 inches use SUBPPROP_UNADJ; MACRPROP_UNADJ is used only when a populated macroplot breakpoint applies. This matches the FIA inventory size-class structure (Burrill et al. 2025). The primary established-tree exposure sums live sugar-maple records with DBH ≥ 5 inches. A separate indicator records whether any live sugar-maple TREE record has DBH 1–4.9 inches. Non-maple basal area remains a competitor covariate and does not treat sugar-maple saplings as non-maple competition.

The calculation is independently checked against FIA COND.BALIVE. Across 1,457 conditions, derived live-tree basal area had Pearson r = 1.000000, mean absolute error 0.000132 ft²/acre, and maximum absolute error 0.000697 ft²/acre.

3.2 Outcome and descriptive screen

The primary response is one when no sugar-maple seedlings were tallied and zero when at least one was tallied. A zero tally is a sampled non-detection, not proof of ecological absence across the condition.

Separately, a transparent descriptive flag identifies sampled conditions with no seedlings tallied and established sugar-maple share in the sample’s upper third. The denominator for that share uses only live trees with DBH ≥ 5 inches. The rule flagged 131 conditions at a cutoff of 51.7%. Because the cutoff is sample-relative, the flag is a screening device and is never used to select the model cohort.

3.3 Primary statistical model

The primary model is a mixed-effects logistic regression with a county random intercept:

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{logit}\{P(Y_c=1)\} =\;& \beta_0 + \beta_1 z\{\log(1 + BA^{est}_{maple,c})\} + \beta_2 SaplingPresent_c \\ &+ \beta_3 z\{\log(1 + BA_{nonmaple,c})\} + \beta_4 z(MicroplotCoverage_c) + \beta_5 Treated_c \\ &+ \beta_6 z(StandAge_c) + \beta_7 Disturbed_c + \beta_8 z(Temperature_c) \\ &+ \beta_9 z(Precipitation_c) + \beta_{10}z(Year_c) + u_{county[c]}. \end{aligned} \]

Continuous predictors are standardized within the final model-complete cohort. The established-tree term is linear on the standardized log1p scale. A two-degree-of-freedom natural spline did not improve fit (likelihood-ratio p = 0.514; ΔAIC = 1.57). Binary sapling presence had the best AIC among no sapling term, continuous sapling basal area, presence, and presence-plus-amount alternatives. Its likelihood-ratio comparison with no sapling term gave p 4.2e-12.

The county-only random structure was retained. The added plot-visit variance was 0.0000, the extended model was singular, and its AIC was 2.0 points higher. The primary fit has 33.3 outcome events per non-intercept fixed coefficient.

3.4 Validation and robustness

Ten deterministic folds assign whole counties, not individual records. Transformations are learned again within every training fold. Held-out counties are predicted using fixed effects only, so no county random effect leaks into assessment predictions. Performance is summarized with Brier score, ROC AUC, calibration intercept, and calibration slope.

Model-based Wald intervals are accompanied by a fixed-effect logistic sensitivity with county-cluster CR1 covariance. Forest type is evaluated through an omnibus adjustment and a restriction to the dominant type 801. Simulation-based DHARMa checks assess uniformity, dispersion, outliers, and residual quantiles. Recorded treatment is included because it is scientifically motivated and complete, but it is interpreted only as an observational covariate.

4.1 Study context

4.2 Descriptive canopy-to-seedling mismatch

The primary cutoff flagged 131 of 1424 sampled conditions (9.2%). Raising the established-tree-share cutoff to the upper quartile reduced the count to 102 (7.2%), confirming that the flag is useful for triage but not a fixed ecological category.

4.3 Cohort continuity

The raw contrast is substantial: 45.7% non-detection without sugar-maple saplings versus 18.1% with them. This is a same-measurement cohort pattern, not an observed transition through time.

4.4 Adjusted associations

At 25 ft²/acre of established sugar-maple basal area and the stated reference profile, predicted non-detection was 45% without saplings and 21% with saplings. The line separation represents the model’s sapling-presence coefficient. The gradual within-line slope represents established-tree basal area, whose interval includes no independent association.

Sapling presence remained the clearest continuity signal (OR 0.33, 95% CI 0.24–0.45). Established-tree basal area was weaker after size-class decomposition (OR 0.90, 95% CI 0.75–1.08). Higher non-maple basal area accompanied greater non-detection odds (OR 1.29), while greater microplot condition coverage accompanied lower odds (OR 0.64), reinforcing the importance of sampling opportunity.

Recorded treatment was associated with lower non-detection odds (OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.32–0.86). Of 1072 primary-cohort conditions, 151 were treatment-flagged. FIA treatment codes summarize heterogeneous recent practices; the most common recorded code was cutting (149 conditions). Without a before-and-after design or untreated counterfactual, the coefficient must not be interpreted as a management effect.

The Daymet coefficients describe broad county gradients, not plot-level climate responses. Mean annual temperature had OR 1.73 (95% CI 1.35–2.20); precipitation had OR 0.80 (95% CI 0.62–1.02).

4.5 Robustness checks

The binary sapling term outperformed a continuous amount term. Adding positive sapling amount after presence increased AIC by 1.50, so the simpler ecological indicator was retained.

The county-cluster CR1 sensitivity gave a sapling-presence OR of 0.30 (95% CI 0.20–0.44). Forest-type indicators did not improve the primary model in an omnibus test (p = 0.545). Restricting to the dominant type 801 left the established-tree direction similar but imprecise (OR 0.93, 95% CI 0.74–1.18). Sparse forest-type cells are not interpreted individually.

Across all 1,072 held-out predictions, calibration intercept was -0.106 and calibration slope was 0.889. All folds retained both outcome classes, and no training fit was singular. The conditional in-sample Brier score was 0.151 and is not presented as geographic generalization performance.

No simulation-based check crossed the pre-specified 0.05 flag: uniformity p = 0.219, dispersion p = 0.800, outlier p = 1.000, and combined residual-quantile p = 0.523. Passing diagnostics do not prove that the model is perfectly specified.

4.7 Broad geographic pattern

Only 17 counties met the minimum of 20 seedling-sampled conditions. Unsupported counties are suppressed rather than assigned unstable fractions. Because only county identifiers are available, the project does not claim a plot-level residual spatial-autocorrelation test.

5 Discussion

What the evidence supports

  • Sugar-maple sapling presence is a strong marker of seedling-stage continuity in the established-tree cohort.
  • Established-tree abundance alone carries limited independent information after the sapling stage is separated.
  • Competition, sampling opportunity, treatment records, and broad county climate add contextual association.
  • The model discriminates reasonably when entire counties are held out.

What it does not establish

  • Seedling-to-sapling or sapling-to-canopy transition probabilities.
  • A causal treatment, climate, canopy, browsing, or competition effect.
  • Statewide prevalence or formal FIA population estimates.
  • Fine-scale spatial mechanisms at confidential FIA plot locations.

The most important analytical lesson is the size-class correction. The full-cohort baseline reproduced a steep relationship between all-size sugar-maple basal area and seedling non-detection. That curve looked compelling but partly reflected sugar-maple saplings inside the predictor and conditions with no established sugar maple at all. Separating established trees from the intermediate cohort changed the scientific story: sapling presence, not a steep mature-tree curve, is the clearest cross-sectional signal.

That is a more useful resilience result. It identifies a measurable cohort bottleneck and shows how data-engineering choices can alter ecological interpretation. It also suggests a direct next study: repeated FIA condition measurements or field follow-up that observes movement from seedlings to saplings while incorporating browsing, soils, and treatment timing.

6 Limitations

  1. Cross-sectional outcome. Size classes are observed at one measurement, so arrows in the conceptual story do not represent observed transitions.
  2. Sampling opportunity. A zero means no seedlings were tallied on sampled microplots, not absence across the entire condition.
  3. Observational treatment. TRTCD records heterogeneous recent treatments and supplies no randomized or before-and-after counterfactual.
  4. Sample versus population. TPA_UNADJ and frame proportions expand records within the plot design; they are not statewide survey weights. Formal estimates require FIA evaluation, stratum, adjustment, and sampling-error procedures (Bechtold and Patterson 2005).
  5. Broad climate proxies. One county interior Daymet cell cannot reproduce plot-level climate or recent weather.
  6. Confidential locations. County aggregation protects plots but prevents fine-scale spatial analysis.
  7. Unmeasured drivers. Browsing, soils, seed supply, competing vegetation, and treatment detail remain incomplete.
  8. Exploratory gap flag. The upper-third rule is sample-relative and threshold-dependent.

Raw FIA data are excluded from version control. The public repository records source URLs, retrieval timestamps, file sizes, and SHA-256 checksums for cached inputs. Join audits record row counts, unique keys, and unmatched keys. Model artifacts include cohort support, scaling constants, functional-form and random-structure comparisons, conventional and county-cluster inference, forest-type sensitivity, validation folds, diagnostics, and the exact data behind the published curves.

From a fresh clone, the analysis runs with:

make setup
make acquire
make all

Package versions are recorded in renv.lock. The release was tested with R 4.6.1 and Quarto 1.9.38. FIA’s state archive URL is mutable, so acquisition checks source size and SHA-256 against the published manifest before analysis.
